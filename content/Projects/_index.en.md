### Why personality psychology?

I've been interested in personality since well before I had frameworks for describing it! I've always enjoyed learning about how other people think and view the world, and studying personality psychology is one way to do that more systematically. I'm very driven by the idea that a lot of conflict and misunderstanding among people can be explained by the assumptions we make about those who behave differently than us. I think learning about personality can turn differences we might initially view with distrust or frustration into a stronger understanding of each other.

I was also drawn to personality research because of how applicable it is to so many topics we care about. Personality psychology offers a very useful lens for understanding behavior as it relates to anything from people's life outcomes (e.g., regarding health, well-being, education), to broader policy or business-related decisions. It also connects easily to other fields across the social sciences and subfields within psychology, which can make for some very interesting interdisciplinary work.  


### How do measurement-related decisions shape the conclusions we draw from our data?

This question has been the throughline of my research, which focuses on how we measure a variety of personality differences. The measurement strategy we choose can influence every step of the research process—-from how we conceptualize a psychological variable to the conclusions we draw about its relationship with outcomes of interest.

My projects have focused on developing public-domain personality assessment tools, exploring the limitations of measurement-related norms and assumptions (e.g., the use of multi-item scales and latent variable modeling approaches), and identifying alternative measurement and data analysis strategies.
